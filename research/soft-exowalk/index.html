SoftEXOwalk: a soft exosuit that senses its own pull

An untethered bilateral knee-flexion exosuit driven by sensorized pneumatic muscles, which estimate the force they deliver without a load cell and use it to personalize assistance in about three minutes per walking speed.

The idea

Soft exosuits are comfortable, but the actuators that make them soft usually cannot tell how hard they are pulling, and tuning them to a person typically needs a metabolic cart and a long session.

SoftEXOwalk tackles both: a pneumatic muscle with its own strain sensor estimates the force it transmits, and that force signal becomes the objective for fast, metabolic-free human-in-the-loop optimization.

01System design

Anatomy of an untethered soft exosuit

A sensorized pneumatic muscle spans the back of each knee and pulls through textile anchors on the thigh and shank to assist knee flexion. A torso brace and shoulder straps route the reaction load into the trunk instead of the skin.

The backpack carries everything else: Li-Po power, two pressure accumulators, proportional regulators and valves for each leg, sensor hubs, and the computers that run control and inference.

From J1SoftEXOwalk · IEEE T-MRB 2026

Textile interface

Webbing that routes force, not pressure points

Velcro-adjusted waist–thigh sleeves, shank anchorages and a torso brace give the muscles firm anchors and spread the reaction load; the suit goes on in a few steps without tools.

02Sensorized pneumatic muscle (sen-pSTAM)

A muscle that measures its own force

Each actuator is a low-pressure silicone-tube muscle cast in a 3D-printed mould and wrapped in a chiffon sheath and braided sleeve. A thin carbon-black/Ecoflex strain sensor, spin-coated and cured in-house, is wrapped around the bladder, so the muscle’s own expansion becomes a signal.

Bladder strain and pressure together encode the axial load, and a lightweight LSTM turns a sliding window of both into the transmitted force. The actuator geometry was chosen with neural surrogate models of the actuator family under gait-derived constraints.

  • Transmitted-force RMSE 3.98 N across 3.5–5.5 km/h, with no external force sensor

From J1SoftEXOwalk · IEEE T-MRB 2026

Learning-based force estimation

From strain to newtons

A tabletop knee emulator replays gait-like stretching while the actuator fires, giving paired strain, pressure and load-cell data to train the estimator, which then runs on the suit.

From J1SoftEXOwalk · IEEE T-MRB 2026

Working principle

Why bladder strain carries the load

Under pressure the bladder bulges against the braid, the braid angle opens and the muscle shortens. How far the bladder can expand depends on the axial load, which is what makes its circumferential strain informative.

03Untethered pneumatics

Air on board, sized by a model

A portable compressor charges a two-stage accumulator system in the backpack. A data-driven performance model sets how much pressure each secondary reservoir must hold for the next actuation, and refilling happens between cycles, so the muscles get a steady supply without a hose to the wall. The architecture is the subject of a US patent application.

For the personalization trials the onboard hardware was worn but air was supplied continuously off-board; the two-stage supply was used for untethered deployment.

04Software

One ROS graph, three ways to run

Sensing, control, inference and optimization run as ROS nodes on three boards: Teensy-based sensor hubs for each leg, a LattePanda for synchronization and high-level control, and a Jetson Xavier for the learned models.

The same graph runs in three modes: deployment from a look-up table of personalized parameters, human-in-the-loop optimization, and deployment with the untethered two-stage supply. Walking speed, estimated from an insole and leg lengths (RMSE 0.142 km/h), selects the right parameters as the wearer speeds up or slows down.

Context for the controller

Knowing when not to assist

An IMU-based classifier separates standing, fidgeting, walking and left and right turns, so the high-level controller can adapt or hold assistance.

05Human-in-the-loop optimization

Personalized in three minutes, without a metabolic cart

Metabolic cost is slow to measure and noisy over short bouts. SoftEXOwalk optimizes on the force it already estimates instead: the transmitted force is split by gait phase into the part that assists knee flexion and the part that resists extension, and the objective rewards the first while constraining the second.

Bayesian optimization tunes the actuation timing and peak pressure of each leg while the person walks. Across ten participants at five treadmill speeds, a single-speed calibration converged in 2.93 ± 0.87 minutes.

06Evaluation

Did it help? The muscles say so

Wireless sEMG on the knee flexors and extensor compared assisted with unassisted walking. Reductions were statistically significant in most participant–speed–leg combinations: 87% of cases for semitendinosus, 90% for biceps femoris and 97% for gastrocnemius medialis.

  • Largest mean reductions: 19.9% semitendinosus, 12.6% biceps femoris, 32.2% gastrocnemius medialis
