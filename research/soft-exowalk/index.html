The project

Overview

SoftEXOwalk brings soft actuation, sensing, control, and human experimentation into an untethered bilateral knee-flexion exosuit. Textile webbing routes forces from custom sensorized pneumatic silicone-tube artificial muscles, while onboard inference estimates transmitted force from the muscles’ embedded strain sensing and pressure feedback. Actuator geometry is designed around the stroke and response demanded by walking.

Its personalization method divides transmitted force according to the gait cycle, rewarding assistance while constraining unwanted resistance. Bayesian optimization tunes each wearer’s control parameters in about three minutes per speed, without a metabolic analyzer in the objective. Multi-subject sEMG and wearability feedback evaluate assistance; these muscle-activity outcomes are not measurements of metabolic-energy savings.

My contributions

  • Worked on the soft-exosuit program from its inception, spanning custom actuator fabrication, sensing, characterization rigs, embedded integration, and human experiments.
  • Developed sensorized pSTAMs and data-driven transmitted-force estimation to connect soft actuation with intrinsic feedback.
  • Integrated textile force routing, wearable sensing, ROS-based control, onboard inference, and portable pneumatics.
  • Developed the transmitted-force-based constrained HIL objective and evaluated personalized bilateral assistance with sEMG.

01

Textile anchoring and system integration

Routing assistance through a soft wearable

The webbing combines waist–thigh sleeves, a torso brace, ankle sleeves, straps, and reinforced force routes. Thigh and shank anchorages position the muscles across the knee, with tendon-length adjustment used to establish the transmission geometry. The structure distributes the reaction forces required for knee flexion while keeping the wearable interface predominantly textile-based.

The complete system adds instrumented insoles, IMUs, pneumatic hardware, batteries, and a central backpack. Its design therefore treats fit, actuator placement, sensing, and onboard mass as one integration problem. Earlier pneumatic assistance experiments established components of this route, while the later SoftEXOwalk study evaluates the complete sensorized bilateral system.

02

Sensorized pSTAM fabrication and design

An artificial muscle that also senses its deformation

The sen-pSTAM combines a custom silicone bladder, a nylon braided sleeve, end fittings, and an integrated carbon-black–Ecoflex strain sensor. The conductive elastomer is prepared and formed as a compliant sensing element, attached to the bladder, and enclosed within the braid. Inflation produces axial contraction while the embedded sensor records deformation.

Neural surrogates and constrained design optimization select actuator geometry using gait-derived length changes, required contraction, and dynamic response. This links fabrication to an application-specific design target rather than choosing a muscle solely by its maximum force. The carbon-black sensor in SoftEXOwalk is one specific embodiment, distinct from earlier liquid-metal and conductive-fabric sensing studies.

03

Onboard LSTM inference

Estimating transmitted force from within the muscle

Pressure and embedded strain feedback are converted into sliding-window features for a lightweight LSTM force estimator. The model captures the time-dependent relationship among pneumatic input, muscle deformation, and transmitted force, supporting inference while the wearer walks. The main study reports an average RMSE of 3.98 N across walking speeds of 3.5–5.5 km/h.

Reference load cells are used to obtain training and validation measurements; the intrinsic sensing pathway then supplies force estimates without requiring an external force sensor for that inference. This distinction matters because the force signal becomes part of a wearable personalization loop, where additional inline instrumentation would otherwise complicate the transmission.

04

Untethered electro-pneumatics

Portable air supply for repeated assistance

Portable pneumatics replenish stored air while meeting the muscles’ pressure and timing requirements. The SoftEXOwalk hardware integrates a compressor, accumulators, valves, regulators and pressure sensing. The main paper describes an alternating-accumulator supply; a separate model-based two-stage configuration is documented in the supplementary material and related patent.

In the two-stage architecture, a primary reservoir replenishes secondary reservoirs, and a data-driven model selects the secondary-pressure target needed for a desired actuator pressure and rise time. Separating recharge from actuator delivery supports repeated pneumatic actions. The alternating-accumulator and two-stage arrangements are distinct configurations within the broader wearable-robotics programme.

05

ROS-based wearable architecture

Connecting sensing, inference, and bilateral actuation

The control architecture coordinates limb sensing, synchronized acquisition, pneumatic commands, force estimation, and personalization. Embedded controllers handle peripheral signals, while host computation and a Jetson provide higher-level processing and edge inference. This distribution allows the soft wearable to combine local I/O with computational models that depend on a recent history of motion and pressure.

A separate speed estimator uses lower-limb anthropometry and insole-derived gait-cycle duration to select previously optimized assistance parameters as speed changes. This model predates Tread’s context-aware IMU representation and has a different input set. ROS provides the integration backbone across sensing, inference and control.

06

A metabolic-free optimization objective

Personalizing force delivery in a few minutes

The HIL method separates assistance from resistance according to the gait cycle, then uses transmitted-force feedback to score candidate control parameters. Constrained Bayesian optimization seeks useful assistance while limiting resistance outside the desired phase. Across ten participants and both legs, single-speed personalization took 2.93 ± 0.87 minutes, with peak assistive forces reaching 42.26 N.

Calibration used a continuous offboard air supply while the onboard hardware remained mounted, keeping the wearable mass present during optimization. Subsequent sEMG comparisons examine muscle-specific responses to personalized assistance. The result is a rapid force-based calibration method; metabolic benefit and fully untethered HIL calibration are separate questions from the reported objective and protocol.
