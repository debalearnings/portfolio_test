Tread: seeing the stairs before the step

A visuo-inertial pipeline that recognises the terrain ahead from a chest camera, identifies the locomotion mode from four IMUs and estimates gait phase and walking speed, all on a body-worn computer.

The idea

An exoskeleton controller needs more than the gait phase. It also needs to know what the person is about to do and what the ground ahead looks like.

Tread links these estimates causally: terrain → locomotion mode → phase and speed. Each stage passes the next a sharper context, instead of running separate terrain-specific models side by side.

My role

  • Developed the terrain-conditioned visuo-inertial inference chain and its wearable ROS 2 / micro-ROS implementation.
  • Connected transfer-learned terrain perception to inertial locomotion experts with fidget rejection, and fed the mode posterior to a shared gait encoder.
  • Evaluated unseen-user estimation and terrain transitions on multi-participant data.

01Wearable sensing

A body-worn rig with an edge brain

A smartphone camera on a chest mount looks at the ground ahead; IMUs on both thighs and shanks track the legs. ESP32 hubs stream over micro-ROS to a Jetson AGX Orin in a backpack, which runs every model on board.

The inertial models use fused orientation angles rather than raw accelerations and rates, which keeps them tolerant to small changes in how the sensors are strapped on.

From U2Tread · arXiv preprint

02Anticipatory terrain perception

Recognising the ground before the foot gets there

A frozen ResNet-18 backbone with a small trainable head sorts each camera frame into level ground, upstairs or downstairs. The training set combines ExoNet images with frames recorded on our own routes, labelled by which terrain dominates the view.

Because the camera looks ahead, a transition is recognised on average 1.46 s before the foot arrives, with 96.3% accuracy on the held-out test set.

Watch it anticipate

03Terrain-aware locomotion-mode identification

Experts that only speak when they are relevant

The terrain estimate gates two LSTM experts: a level-ground expert that separates standing, fidgeting, walking and turning, and a stairs expert for ascent and descent. Treating fidgeting as its own class keeps small shuffles from being read as the start of a step.

  • 93.7% accuracy for the level-ground expert and 98.8% for the stairs expert
  • Mean transition latency below 200 ms

04Context-aware phase and speed

Context as a probability, not a switch

Hard switching between mode-specific models produces jumps exactly at the transitions that matter. Tread keeps the locomotion posterior as a probabilistic context vector and feeds it, with the IMU features, into one shared LSTM gait encoder. Two decoders read the same representation: bilateral gait phase at 50 Hz and walking speed at 2 Hz.

The terrain gate also let us combine level-ground and stair datasets collected under separate protocols without mixing their labels.

  • Gait-phase RMSE 1.75% (level walking), 2.97% (stair ascent), 4.55% (stair descent), leave-one-subject-out
  • Walking-speed RMSE 0.218 ± 0.072 km/h across 3.5–5.5 km/h

From U2Tread · arXiv preprint

05Out of the lab

On the road, with speed

The same pipeline runs outdoors on a footpath, estimating bilateral gait phase and walking speed on the body-worn computer with no external sensing.

Why the chain matters

One causal chain from what the camera sees to what the legs are doing, so assistance can be adapted before the terrain changes rather than after.
