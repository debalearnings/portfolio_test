Soft actuators that report their own state

Three ways of giving a pneumatic muscle its own sense of stretch and force: liquid metal, conductive fabric and carbon-black silicone, ending in a learned estimator that replaces the load cell.

The idea

A wearable that cannot measure what it delivers has to trust a model or carry a load cell in the force path. Soft actuators make this harder: they stretch, bulge and slide against the body.

This line of work embeds stretchable sensors in the actuator itself and asks which sensing material and placement make the force observable.

My role

  • Developed liquid-metal, conductive-fabric and carbon-black sensing for pneumatic muscles, with their characterization rigs.
  • Compared mounting strategies and built force-estimation algorithms validated against in-line load cells.
  • Brought the approach into SoftEXOwalk with an LSTM estimator.

01Liquid-metal sensing

A serpentine of liquid metal

An EGaIn-filled microchannel in silicone changes resistance as it stretches. Circumferential and axial mountings on the muscle were compared to find a usable response, and a two-step algorithm estimates the force the muscle passes to the knee or ankle during walking, checked against an in-line load cell.

From C6Liquid-metal sensing · ICRAIE 2020

02Conductive fabric

A cheaper path: stretchable fabric

Variable-resistance nylon–spandex fabric needs no microfluidics. Characterized in many cut sizes, the best elements were joined in series along the muscle with inextensible fabric between them. Embedded in silicone, a fabric strain sensor stayed monotonic up to about 71% strain.

From C8Fabric-sensorized PAM · ICECA 2020C11Fabric strain sensor · IOP 2020

03Carbon-black / Ecoflex

A sensor that is part of the bladder

For SoftEXOwalk the sensor became a thin carbon-black/Ecoflex film, spin-coated, oven-cured, trimmed and wrapped around the bladder before the sleeve goes on. It shares the actuator’s materials and moves with it, so there is no mismatch at the interface.

Characterized on a motorized linear stage, the sensor shows hysteresis, one reason the final estimator works on a time window rather than single readings.

From J1SoftEXOwalk · IEEE T-MRB 2026

04From signal to force

Let a network undo the hysteresis

Bladder strain alone is ambiguous; strain and pressure together determine the axial load. A lightweight LSTM reads sliding windows of both and estimates transmitted force across walking speeds, which made it the feedback signal for personalization in SoftEXOwalk.

  • Force RMSE 3.98 N across 3.5–5.5 km/h with no external force sensor
