Touch, one pixel at a time

A 3×3 array of pneumatic tactors in a single cast elastomer block, designed with finite-element analysis to deliver spatial touch patterns to the fingertip.

The idea

Surgeons using laparoscopic or collaborative robots lose the sense of touch. A fingertip display that can show where and how something presses gives part of it back.

Built during a research visit to the Neuro-Robotic Touch Laboratory in Pisa, this display packs nine independently pressurized chambers into one cast block. It became the starting point for the fingertip arrays on the soft haptic glove.

My role

  • Designed and fabricated the pneumatic elastomeric tactor array with the Neuro-Robotic Touch team.
  • Used finite-element analysis and experiments to relate material and pressure to tactor deformation.
  • Ran the preliminary psychophysical study of spatial stimuli.

01Design and fabrication

Nine chambers, one cast block

Nine cylindrical chambers, 3 mm in diameter, sit under a thin membrane in a single polymeric block cast in a three-part mould. Pressurizing a chamber lifts its membrane into a small dome against the fingertip.

From C2Tactile display · MetroInd4.0 2020

02FEA and characterization

Material by simulation, pressure by test

Finite-element analysis compared tactor deflection for three candidate elastomers before casting, and free-deflection tests on the array set the working pressure range. A valve array driven from a PC switches each chamber.

From C2Tactile display · MetroInd4.0 2020

03Psychophysics

Which pattern did you feel?

Four participants identified four spatial patterns presented at 35, 70 and 105 kPa. The middle pressure worked best, with 96.9% correct detection.
