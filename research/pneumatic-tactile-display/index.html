The project

Overview

During my visiting research at Sant’Anna’s Neuro-Robotic Touch Laboratory, I worked on a pneumatic fingertip display intended to convey spatial haptic information. A 3×3 array of elastomeric tactors produces localized skin deformation through independently controlled pneumatic stimulation.

The development combines material and finite-element study, fabricated-array characterization, and preliminary psychophysical experiments on stimulus perception. Surgical and collaborative robotics motivate the interface, while the reported evidence concerns the fingertip display and its perceptual characterization. This earlier 3×3 system is distinct from the later soft glove’s 2×2 fingertip arrays.

My contributions

  • Designed and developed the pneumatic elastomeric tactor array during the Sant’Anna research visit with the Neuro-Robotic Touch team.
  • Used finite-element analysis and experimental characterization to relate material and pressure to tactor deformation.
  • Characterized spatial-stimulus perception through preliminary psychophysical experiments.

01

Elastomer design and finite-element analysis

A soft array of individually addressable tactors

Nine pneumatic tactors form a 3×3 fingertip array. Local inflation creates skin deformation at selected positions, allowing the display to encode a spatial pattern rather than a single global vibration or pressure cue.

Material comparison and finite-element analysis examine the relationship between pressure and displacement, while the fabricated array provides a physical check on the chosen design. The mold, completed prototype, and inflated sequence make the link between geometry, manufacturing, and tactile output visible.

02

Independent tactor stimulation

Turning pneumatic patterns into spatial touch

The pneumatic control system selects tactors and pressure levels to render spatial stimuli at the fingertip. The supplied hardware video shows actual array inflation and sequential activation, providing a close-up view of how local pneumatic deformation becomes the display’s output.

The close-up demonstration shows the elastomer rising locally and the spatial pattern changing as tactors are selected. The standalone Sant’Anna array has its own 3×3 layout and pneumatic implementation, distinct from the later 2×2 arrays integrated into the multimodal glove.

03

Preliminary fingertip psychophysics

Testing whether spatial stimuli are distinguishable

The preliminary perception experiment tests four spatial stimuli at 35, 70, and 105 kPa with four participants. Comparing responses across pressure levels examines whether a larger mechanical stimulus necessarily produces better identification.

The best reported identification performance is 96.9% at 70 kPa within this small study. The result supports the feasibility of distinguishable pneumatic spatial cues under the tested conditions, while the limited participant group and stimulus set remain part of its interpretation. It does not establish clinical benefit or the performance of a complete surgical teleoperation system.
