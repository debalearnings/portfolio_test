Air on board: a two-stage supply for soft robots

A light accumulator architecture that lets several pneumatic actuators fire quickly without a hose to the wall, with a data-driven model deciding how much pressure to keep in reserve.

The idea

Pneumatic muscles are light; the air that drives them usually is not. A compressor sized for peak flow is heavy, and a single tank loses pressure with every actuation.

The two-stage architecture separates storage from delivery. Lightweight PET-bottle accumulators hold the air; small secondary reservoirs are pre-charged to exactly what the next actuation needs and refilled between cycles.

My role

  • Co-invented the two-stage accumulator architecture and its data-driven performance model.
  • Built and integrated it in the SoftEXOwalk backpack and adapted it as the pneumatic battery of the aerial soft gripper.

01How it works

Store, stage, deliver

Primary reservoirs feed secondary reservoirs in series; PWM-controlled valves release air from each secondary reservoir into its actuator. A performance model built from data predicts the secondary pressure needed to reach a target actuator pressure within a target time.

Between actuation cycles a pressure-feedback PD loop recharges the secondary reservoirs, so the next stroke starts from the right state.

02Application: SoftEXOwalk

A backpack that breathes for two legs

In SoftEXOwalk the supply sits in the backpack with a portable compressor, two accumulators, pressure sensors and a valve group per leg. The ROS graph has a dedicated deployment mode that runs the exosuit from this supply.

During personalization trials air was supplied continuously off-board; the two-stage supply was used for untethered deployment.

From J1

03Application: aerial manipulation

The same idea, in the air

On the aerial manipulator, a PET-bottle pneumatic battery and a small secondary chamber drive a three-finger soft gripper, giving about twenty grasps per charge with no compressor on the drone.
