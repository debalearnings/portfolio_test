pSTAM: pneumatic muscles, made to measure

Silicone-tube artificial muscles that work at low pressure, built in-house so their size can follow the task, then characterized, modelled with neural surrogates and controlled with learning-assisted schemes.

Fabrication

The idea

Commercial McKibben muscles come in a few sizes and need high pressure. For wearables the actuator should match the joint, the stroke and the force the task needs, and run from a small supply.

pSTAMs make that possible: a cast silicone bladder, a protective chiffon sheath and a braided sleeve, assembled with simple fittings, in whatever length and diameter the application calls for.

My role

  • Developed the custom fabrication process and systematic static and dynamic characterization.
  • Built test rigs and application emulators linking geometry, operating conditions, force, contraction and response.
  • Developed neural surrogate models, constrained actuator design and NN-assisted feedback control.

01Construction

A muscle you can cast on a bench

The bladder is Ecoflex silicone cast around a metal core in a 3D-printed mould and degassed under vacuum. A chiffon sheath protects it from the braid, the braided sleeve converts radial bulging into axial contraction, and PET ties and Kevlar thread close the ends around standard pneumatic connectors.

From P2

A family, not a part

Sizes that follow the task

Lengths, diameters and wall thicknesses were varied to build a whole family, which made it possible to learn how geometry maps to behaviour.

From J3

Fabrication process

Mould, mix, degas, cast, assemble

The process uses commercially available materials and desktop tools, so a new size can be made in a day. Two versions were studied in depth: a 230 mm long muscle (82 g) for ankle assistance and a 130 mm short one (46 g) for knee and ankle assistance during walking.

From J4

02Static and dynamic characterization

Measuring more than a force–pressure curve

A vertical rig with a load cell, encoder-coupled motor and proportional regulator runs isobaric loading–unloading cycles, stiffness tests and step responses under constant load or fixed length. The rig and its air-consumption measurement are the subject of a second patent application.

The data fed a three-element phenomenological model (spring, damper, contractile element) for dynamics and corrected analytical models for the quasi-static response.

03Behavioural modelling and design

Learning the muscle, then designing it

Eight ANN models, trained with metaheuristic optimizers on the characterization data, predict static and dynamic behaviour from geometry and operating conditions. Particle swarm optimization then searched for a knee-assist muscle that meets a stroke limit from gait biomechanics and fast enough inflation and deflation.

The result, 151.6 mm long and 14.5 mm in diameter at 163.2 kPa, was tested on a knee emulator that replays the distance between the thigh and shank anchor points during walking.

From J3

04Learning-assisted control

Taming the nonlinearity with a network

A pSTAM-driven robotic joint was controlled by a neural network that supplies the feedforward pressure for a target angle and load, with a PD loop correcting the residual error. It tracked reference angles better than open loop and recovered when the load changed mid-task.

A related scheme schedules PID gains with a network across load and operating region.

From C3, C15

05Evaluation on people

From the bench to the treadmill

Two short muscles across the ankle, triggered by a sensorized insole at heel strike, assisted plantarflexion during level walking; a second activity assisted seated ankle plantarflexion against a load. Surface EMG on seven leg muscles of five participants showed lower activity with assistance. This line of work led directly to SoftEXOwalk.
