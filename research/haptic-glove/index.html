A soft glove that lets you feel the virtual

Custom bend sensors track the fingers, pneumatic fingertip arrays render where and how an object touches, and a motor–tendon system pushes back with the object’s stiffness.

The idea

Most VR gloves either track the hand well or give convincing feedback, rarely both in a light, soft package. This glove keeps each function in its own compact module so the fingers stay free to move.

Contact location and surface direction are rendered at the skin by small pneumatic tactors; size, shape and stiffness come from resistance in the tendons. The same glove later drove an Allegro robot hand in teleoperation.

My role

  • Developed the flexible finger-sensing and multimodal feedback modules within the soft-glove project.
  • Integrated pneumatic cutaneous rendering with motor–tendon kinesthetic feedback for virtual contact and stiffness.
  • Connected glove signals, wrist tracking, Unity interaction and feedback control over ROS, and ran the psychophysical studies.

01System overview

Three modules, one soft glove

Thumb, index and middle fingers each carry two bend sensors, a 2×2 tactor bay at the tip and a tendon routed to a motor–pulley unit off the hand. A Vive tracker gives wrist pose; valves, regulators and motor drivers sit on the bench and talk to the Unity scene.

02Proprioceptive sensing

Bend sensors cut to fit the joint

The sensors are laminated stacks of copper electrodes and a carbon-impregnated Velostat film. Nine lengths and widths were built and characterized, and the design was chosen by optimization for sensitivity, usable range and response time.

  • Sensitivity 27.5 Ω/° over a usable 16.7–85.3° bend
  • Rise and decay times of about 0.4 s

From J2

03Cutaneous feedback

Fingertip pixels you can feel

Each fingertip wears four silicone tactors in a conformal bay. Pressurized through solenoid valves, a tactor bulges 0.68 mm at 100 kPa within about 0.2 s and pushes with up to 0.9 N; patterns of active tactors render where an object touches and which way a surface slides.

From J2

04Kinesthetic feedback

Resistance through a tendon

When a virtual finger meets an object, a motor reels in that finger’s tendon. A cascaded position–current loop makes the motor behave like a spring of chosen stiffness, so a soft ball and a hard cube push back differently. Three stiffness levels were rendered, from 31 to 156 N·mm/rad at the motor.

From J2

05Psychophysical evaluation

Can people tell? Yes, quickly

Ten participants grasped cylinders, cubes and spheres, located contacts, judged sliding directions and sorted objects by stiffness. Grasp time fell from 16.2 s to 12.5 s across ten trials, and perceived workload was measured with NASA-TLX.

  • Contact-area detection about 97.5% (finger extended)
  • Directional stimuli recognised with F1 scores of 0.87–0.97
  • Stiffness sorting precision 0.87–0.95 across three levels
